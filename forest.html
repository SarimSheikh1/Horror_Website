The Cursed Forest

You entered at dusk. The trees closed in.
There is no path back. There is only deeper.

"The Grimvale Forest has swallowed 23 people since 1970. Searchers found no bodies — only trails that ended mid-path, as though the walkers simply ceased to exist."

— LOCAL LEGEND, GRIMVALE COUNTY

7:43 PM · 0.3 miles in

The Path Splits

The trail forks without warning. Both paths look identical — same width, same undergrowth, same canopy of black branches overhead. You check your phone. No signal. The GPS shows only a grey blank where the forest should be. A light breeze stirs the leaves. And then, from the left path, you hear something. A sound that takes you three full seconds to identify. Someone crying.

8:11 PM · 0.9 miles in

The Hanging Things

You find them hanging from the branches — dozens of them. Small bundles of sticks and string, tied into figures that are almost human. They cover a clearing perhaps twenty metres wide, spinning slowly in the still air. No wind moves them. In the centre of the clearing, a circle of stones around a long-dead fire. Among the ash, you see something white. Bones. Small ones. Child-sized.

9:04 PM · 2.1 miles in

The Cabin

A log cabin, far from any road, light glowing warm through a single window. On the porch, a rocking chair moves slowly back and forth. The chair is empty. The movement is not caused by wind. A sign above the door reads, in fresh white paint: "SHELTER · ALL ARE WELCOME". Beneath it, someone has scratched in the wood with a knife: "do not go inside."

10:47 PM · ??? miles in

You've Been Walking In Circles

You recognise the clearing. The hanging figures. The stone circle. But you entered from a different direction this time. You have been walking straight — you checked with your compass — and yet you are back where you started. The bones in the fire circle are gone. The rocking chair from the cabin is here now, in the middle of the clearing, still rocking. There is a figure in it. The figure is looking at you. It has been here the whole time, watching you walk your circles. It slowly raises one hand. It points — not at you — but at something directly behind you.

Very slowly. Turn around.
